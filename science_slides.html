The Question
I walk past a pond every morning and it always feels damp.

My homelab feels like a furnace.

But are my senses right — or am I just imagining it?
I put a DHT11 sensor at three spots — at a pond, in my homelab, and in a hallway. Five minutes each. Temperature and humidity. Real numbers, not feelings.
Hypothesis
If...
a DHT11 sensor is placed in three different microenvironments for 5 minutes each...
Then...
the readings will show that areas near water have higher humidity, and areas near electronics have higher temperature, compared to a neutral indoor location.

Because water evaporates into the surrounding air, increasing humidity near the pond, and electronics convert electrical energy into waste heat, raising local temperature.

Research Question
How do temperature and humidity readings vary across three different microenvironments — outdoor near water, indoor near electronics, and a neutral indoor location — over equal 5-minute sampling periods?

The Code
sensor_logger.py 
import board, time, csv
from datetime import datetime
import adafruit_dht

# Initialize DHT11 sensor on GPIO pin D4
sensor = adafruit_dht.DHT11(board.D4)

LOCATION = "pond_edge"
DURATION = 300  # 5 minutes in seconds
INTERVAL = 10   # reading every 10s

with open(f"data_{LOCATION}.csv", "w") as f:
    writer = csv.writer(f)
    writer.writerow(["timestamp", "temp_C", "humidity_%"])
    
    start = time.time()
    while time.time() - start < DURATION:
        writer.writerow([
            datetime.now().isoformat(),
            round(sensor.temperature, 1),
            round(sensor.humidity, 1)
        ])
        time.sleep(INTERVAL)
    
    print(✅ Data collection complete)

Hypothesis
Supported — water raised humidity, electronics raised temperature

Limitations & Sources of Error
Sensor Accuracy
DHT11 has ±2°C and ±5% RH margin of error. Impact: the real pond temperature could be as low as 15.8°C or as high as 19.8°C. That is a big range when you are trying to compare locations.
Short Sampling
Only 5 minutes per location. Impact: if a cloud passed over or a server fan kicked in during those 5 minutes, the data would look very different. A longer window would smooth that out.
Single Time Point
All tests done in one 20-minute window. Impact: morning sun, afternoon heat, and evening cool all change microclimates. One snapshot does not show the full picture.
What I Would Do Differently
Sample for 30+ minutes at each location. Repeat on multiple days. Add more spots — like near a window or outside in direct sun. And bring a backup sensor.
Being honest about limitations is part of science — it shows you understand the method, not just the result.
When Things Go Wrong
The DHT11 sensor module broke before the demo.
What Happened
I tried to solder the DHT11 sensor to a header so it could plug into the Pi. The sensor module stopped working after that. It would not give any readings.
The Fix
I used the OpenWeatherMap API to get the current outdoor temperature instead. It is not the same as a live sensor, but it still shows the Pi can display real weather data.
What I Learned
Soldering matters. A bad connection can kill a whole component. Next time I would use a breadboard first to test before making anything permanent.
The Unicorn HAT Mini display still works — it shows weather, time, and humidity. Just not from a live sensor anymore.
Why It Matters
Microclimates affect comfort, energy use,
and even hardware lifespan.
Server rooms that overheat cost companies millions in cooling. A 9.5°C swing between two rooms in the same building shows why sensor placement matters. Even a 2°C difference affects hardware failure rates.

Greenhouses need 60-80% humidity for most crops — our pond edge hit 82%. Homes need 30-50% for comfort and to prevent mold — our hallway sat at 47%.

Understanding microclimates with real data helps us make better decisions about where to put things — from plants to computers to people.
